Microservices Cheat Sheet

The compressed essence of the course. Skim before a lesson, study it fully before an interview.

1. Definition

An approach to developing a single application as a suite of small services, each running in its own process and communicating with lightweight mechanisms… built around business capabilities and independently deployable.Fowler & Lewis — Microservices
  • Defining traits: componentization via services, business capability, decentralized data, smart endpoints / dumb pipes, design for failure.1
  • Independent deployment is the price of admission; coordinated deploys ⇒ not (really) microservices.2

2. Trade-offs

BenefitCost
Strong module boundaries (large teams)Distribution — remote calls are slow and can fail
Independent deploymentEventual consistency — strong consistency is hard
Technology diversityOperational complexity — many services to run

Microservice premium: the style costs productivity that only more complex systems repay. If a monolith manages your complexity, keep it.2

3. Finding boundaries

  • Decompose by business capability / subdomain (a service owns one).3
  • Conway's Law: team structure shapes the architecture — size services to team ownership.2
  • Beware the distributed monolith: services that must change together.

4. Communication

  • Smart endpoints, dumb pipes: keep logic in services, pipes simple.1
  • Sync (HTTP) for request/response; async (events) to decouple and tolerate failure.1
  • Synchronous coupling between services is often discouraged — prefer asynchronous/retryable flows.1

5. Data & consistency

  • Database per service: each service owns its data; a shared DB couples services.3
  • Accept eventual consistency; strong consistency across services is extremely difficult.2
  • Patterns: saga (sequence of local transactions + compensations), transactional outbox (publish events reliably), API composition, CQRS.45

6. Edge, resilience, operations

  • API gateway — one entry that routes and composes; keep clients off the mesh.6
  • Design for failure: timeouts, retries (idempotent handlers), circuit breaker, service discovery.7
  • Observe: distributed tracing + metrics across calls; infrastructure automation for deploys.

7. Decision checklist

  • If a well-structured monolith controls complexity, stay with it (premium).2
  • Split when teams or scaling requires it — align services to business capabilities and team ownership.
  • Keep each service's data private; use async/events and sagas where a single ACID tx is impossible.
  • Put a gateway at the edge; make failures visible and self-healing.
  • Migrate a monolith incrementally (strangler fig), never a big-bang rewrite.1
