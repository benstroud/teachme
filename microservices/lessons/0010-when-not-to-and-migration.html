When Not To & Migration

Microservices are a decision, not a default — and when you do split, do it one capability at a time, never a big-bang rewrite.

The mental model in one sentence

Choose microservices only when their premium is repaid (team size, scaling, independent cadence), and when you do migrate, replace the monolith incrementally — the strangler fig approach — rather than rewriting it wholesale.21

When NOT to split

  • Small team, manageable monolith — if a well-structured monolith contains the complexity, the premium is not worth paying.2
  • No independent deploy need — the whole point is missing if services must deploy together (that is a distributed monolith).2
  • Immature operations — many services demand automation and observability that a small team may not yet have.2

Migrating with the strangler fig

Extract one business capability at a time into a new service, routing traffic to it while the monolith handles the rest — then repeat. This keeps the system shipping and rollbackable at each step, avoiding the risk of a big-bang rewrite.1

Key idea The style's soft factors — team quality, collaboration, domain communication — matter more than the split itself. Choose microservices for capability and team reasons, not because they sound modern.
