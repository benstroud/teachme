Finding Service Boundaries

The hardest question is "where do services split?" — align each service to a business capability and to a team that owns it.

The mental model in one sentence

A service should own one business capability (subdomain), and its boundary should line up with the team that lives behind it — because structure follows the organization (Conway's Law).32

Decompose by business capability

microservices.io frames it clearly: decompose the application into services, where each service implements one or more subdomains/business capabilities — "an implementable model of a slice of business functionality."3 A service owns its business logic, its data, and its adapters.

Let the team shape the boundary

Conway's Law observes that a system mirrors the communication structure of the organization that built it. With larger or multi-location teams, inter-team communication is less frequent and more formal — so a useful consequence is designing each service so it can be owned by one team.2

Key idea Two heuristics go together: cut along capabilities, not along layers (don't split "front-end", "business logic", "DB" into separate services), and size services so a team can own it end to end. If several teams must change a service together, the boundary is wrong or the service is too big.
