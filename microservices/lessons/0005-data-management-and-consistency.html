Data Management & Consistency

Decentralized data is the price of independent services — each owns its store, and consistency becomes eventual.

The mental model in one sentence

The style is decentralized data management: each service owns its own database, so a shared database is an anti-pattern that couples services — and the flip side is you must accept eventual consistency across services.12

Database per service

Each service owns the data that belongs to its business capability. That makes the service independently schema-managed and lets each team choose its own storage. Sharing a single database between services is generally a trap: it reintroduces the coupling the style exists to remove.3

Why strong consistency is hard

A single database can give you ACID. Across independently owned databases there is no common transaction — so "strong consistency is extremely difficult" and the system moves to eventual consistency: data converges over time rather than being strongly consistent at every instant.2

Key idea You can have ACID within a service or eventual consistency across services — not both. Design users and flows to tolerate the gap (e.g. "we'll confirm shipping shortly") rather than pretending a distributed transaction exists.
