What is a Microservice

Not "many tiny services" for its own sake — a suite of small, independently deployable services, each aligned to a business capability.

The mental model in one sentence

Microservices is an approach to developing one application as a suite of small services, each in its own process, communicating over lightweight mechanisms, built around a business capability, and independently deployable.1

The defining traits

An approach to developing a single application as a suite of small services, each running in its own process and communicating with lightweight mechanisms, often an HTTP resource API. These services are built around business capabilities and independently deployable by fully automated deployment machinery.Fowler & Lewis — Microservices

The frequently cited characteristics of the style include:1

  • Componentization via services — components are services, not in-process libraries.
  • Organized around business capabilities — the service maps to a business function.
  • Decentralized data management — each service owns its own data.
  • Smart endpoints, dumb pipes — logic lives in services; the pipe stays simple.
  • Design for failure and infrastructure automation — the services assume the network/process can fail.
Key idea The two words to hold onto are independently deployable and business capability. If services must be deployed or changed in lockstep, they are not really microservices — they are a distributed monolith.2
