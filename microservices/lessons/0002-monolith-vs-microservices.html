Monolith vs Microservices

These are two regions of architecture, not good versus evil — the microservice premium decides which you can afford.

The mental model in one sentence

A monolith is one deployable unit with strong internal modules; microservices buy strong boundaries and independent deployment at the cost of distribution, consistency, and operations.12

The benefits

  • Strong module boundaries — separates code so changes stay local; especially valuable as teams grow.2
  • Independent deployment — a simple service is easier to deploy and less likely to take down the whole system.2
  • Technology diversity — different languages, frameworks, and stores per service.2

The costs

CostWhat it means
DistributionRemote calls are slow and always at risk of failure.2
Eventual consistencyStrong consistency across services is extremely difficult.2
Operational complexityYou need a mature ops culture to run many redeployed services.2
Key idea The honest framing is the microservice premium: microservices impose a productivity cost that only more complex systems repay. If a well-structured monolith can manage your complexity, you should probably keep it.2

Not a binary choice

Neither is a single fixed thing. A well-structured monolith is not a "Big Ball of Mud," and microservices are not automatically better — treat them as two points in the space of architectures and choose for your context.2
