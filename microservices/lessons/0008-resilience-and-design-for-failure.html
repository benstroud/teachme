Resilience & Design for Failure

A design-for-failure system assumes calls can lag or fail — and uses timeouts, retries, idempotency, and circuit breakers to stay useful.

The mental model in one sentence

Because the network and other services will fail, design-for-failure means every call gets a timeout, failure is retried only when safe (idempotency), and a circuit breaker stops hammering a failing service.12

Idempotency and retries

A retry is dangerous unless the handler is idempotent — a repeated call must not duplicate side effects (e.g. charge twice). Design requests with stable IDs so a retried operation can be recognized and deduplicated.

Circuit breaker

Rather than letting every call wait on a failing service (and degrade the caller), a circuit breaker trips after repeated failures: subsequent calls fail fast for a while, giving the dependency time to recover, then the circuit tries to close again.2

call dependent
  ├─ success  → keep going
  └─ failures → after N, OPEN the circuit: fail fast
                (periodically TRY to close / half-open)
Key idea Resilience is an explicit program, not hope: timeouts bound the wait, idempotency makes retries safe, the breaker contains the blast radius — and observability (next lesson) tells you which one to pay attention to.
