Lesson 4 of 10Microservices

Communication

Services connect over a network — so what they send and how they handle failure is a core design decision: sync or async.

The mental model in one sentence

The style favors smart endpoints and dumb pipes: keep the transport simple, put logic in the services, and choose between synchronous request/response and asynchronous messages/events for each interaction.1

Smart endpoints, dumb pipes

Keep the messaging mechanism (the "pipe") as simple as possible and put the business logic in the services (the "endpoints"). Avoid embedding intelligence in the transport or the message bus.1

Sync vs async

StyleTypical choiceCharacter
Synchronous (e.g. HTTP request/response)Direct reads and calls that need an immediate answerSimple to reason about, but couples the caller to the callee's availability.
Asynchronous (messages / events)Decoupled work, propagation of changesCaller and callee don't wait on each other; better failure tolerance.
Key idea Fowler & Lewis explicitly discuss synchronous calls considered harmful: heavy synchronous coupling between services undermines the decoupling that makes the style valuable. Prefer asynchronous, retryable interactions — publish an event, don't hold a long far-flung call chain together.
