Distillation and Large-Scale Structure

Not every part of a system deserves the same effort. Distillation finds the core domain, where the competitive value lies, and gives it the best modelling. Large-scale structure then organises the whole system around that core.

Distillation

The chapter Distillation in Part IV asks a practical question: which parts of the model are the heart of the business? Evans separates the domain into the core domain, which the business builds for competitive advantage, and the rest. The rest is either generic, meaning the same kind of thing many businesses need, or supporting, meaning it is necessary but not distinctive.1

KindWhat it isWhere effort goes
Core domainWhat makes the business distinctiveYour best modelling, your best people
SupportingNecessary, specific to you, not a differentiatorAdequate, simple, and replaceable
GenericThe same problem as everyone elseBought or adopted, not modelled from scratch

A payroll calculation is often generic, a pricing rule that makes your business unusual may be the core, and a product catalogue may be supporting. The same subject can be core in one company and generic in another, so the decision depends on the business, not the topic.

Large-scale structure

The chapter Large-Scale Structure describes how to organise a whole system once its parts are known. Evans uses the idea of a system metaphor: a shared picture that helps people understand how the parts fit, and responsibility layers that keep the structure from tangling.1 The structure is not a fixed architecture. It is a set of agreed principles that let a team change one part without breaking the whole.

Effort follows the core The practical rule is to spend the most design effort on the core domain, and to keep the rest simple enough that nobody is tempted to over-model it. Over-modelling a supporting subdomain is a common and expensive mistake.

Check yourself

What is the core domain, in this lesson's terms?

The core domain is the distinctive part of the business. It is where competitive advantage lies, so it gets the best modelling.1

A generic subdomain is best handled by which approach?

A generic subdomain is a problem many businesses share. Effort is better spent adopting a proven solution than modelling it in depth.

Why is over-modelling a supporting subdomain a mistake?

Effort is limited. Spending it on a supporting area takes it away from the core, where it matters most.
