Bounded Contexts and Context Maps

One model cannot serve a whole large system. Evans divides the system into contexts, each with a model that is unified inside its boundary, and then maps how the contexts relate.

Why one model is not enough

Part IV, Strategic Design, starts from a plain claim: total unification of the domain model for a large system will not be feasible or cost-effective. The book's answer is to divide the system into bounded contexts, each with its own unified model, and to be explicit about how the contexts relate.21

Fowler reads this as the core of the strategic part of DDD. The same word means different things in different parts of a system, and the boundary is often drawn where the language changes.2

A bounded context

A bounded context is the boundary within which one model applies and the ubiquitous language has one meaning. Inside it, customer means one thing. Across the boundary, customer may mean something else, and the two contexts need a translation.

SalesA prospect or account with a pipelineOpportunities and discounts
BillingA party that receives invoicesPayment terms and tax

The context map

A context map is a picture of the contexts and the relationships between them. Evans names several relationships in Part IV. Among them are the shared kernel, where two contexts share a small part of the model, the customer/supplier relationship, the conformist, and the anticorruption layer, which protects one model from the meaning of another. Others include the open host service and the published language.1

Choose the relationship from the real power balance between teams, not from the diagram you would like. A conformist relationship is often the honest answer when the other team will not change.

The boundary is a team decision Contexts are usually drawn by where the language changes and where a team works. A context that spans three teams with three languages is probably two contexts.

Your task (one attempt)

  1. Pick a large system you know. List three contexts, and for each, the one word that means something different across the boundary.
  2. For one pair of contexts, choose a relationship from this lesson. Justify it using who can change what.
  3. Say where the translation between the two contexts should live, and why it should not live inside either model.

Check yourself

What is a bounded context?

A bounded context is a boundary of model and language, not a deployment unit or a folder. Inside it, a term has one meaning.2

What does a context map show?

A context map depicts the bounded contexts and the relationships between them. It is a strategic picture, not an infrastructure diagram.

Why does the anticorruption layer matter in this lesson?

The anticorruption layer translates between models so the meaning of another context does not leak into yours.1
