Lesson 1 of 10Large Language Models

What is an LLM

At its core an LLM is not a database or an oracle — it is a very large model trained to predict the next token, generation after generation.

The mental model in one sentence

A large language model is a neural network trained on massive amounts of text to predict the next token, and it generates text by repeatedly sampling that next token given everything before it.12

What "language model" means

A language model assigns probabilities to sequences of words. Its workhorse operation is next-token prediction: given a context ("The quick brown…"), output a probability distribution over what comes next. Generation chains this one token at a time.2

What "large" buys you

  • Scale: model size (parameter count) has grown roughly 10x every year — a trend described as "a new Moore's Law."1
  • Capability: bigger models trained on more text capture more of the patterns in language, enabling zero-shot and few-shot behavior on tasks they were not explicitly trained to do.3
  • Cost: that scale is expensive — training and running them demands serious GPUs, which is why most people use pretrained models rather than train their own.1
Key idea Everything an LLM "knows" is encoded in its weights from training on text — not a trusted fact store. Understanding this is what makes responsible use (grounding, evaluation) click into place later.
