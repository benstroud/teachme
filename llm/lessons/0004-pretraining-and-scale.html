Lesson 4 of 10Large Language Models

Pretraining & Scale

Train once on raw text, reuse everywhere: pretraining gives general ability, fine-tuning adapts it — and scale has driven the whole field.

The mental model in one sentence

A model is pretrained on huge amounts of raw text to learn general language ability, then fine-tuned on a narrower task — transfer learning — and the reason this works at all is the sheer scale of data, compute, and parameters.1

Pretraining vs fine-tuning

  • Pretraining — minimal objective (predict the next token) over massive text; produces a general-purpose base model.
  • Fine-tuning — continue training a pretrained checkpoint on task data so it adapts (e.g. to follow instructions).
  • Transfer learning — reuse what was learned on the big general corpus rather than training from scratch.1
Key idea "Architecture" is the network's structure; a checkpoint is the specific weights loaded into it. When people say a model like GPT or Llama, they usually mean a published checkpoint you can fine-tune or query.2

An architecture timeline

GPT (2018, first pretrained Transformer for fine-tuning) → BERT (2018) → GPT-2 (2019) → T5 (2019) → GPT-3 (2020, zero-shot) → InstructGPT (2022, instruction-following) → Llama (2023) → Mistral (2023).2

Scale drives the curve

Model size has grown roughly 10x every year, making language models look like "a new Moore's Law." That scale is why the practical path is to use pretrained models and fine-tune, and why optimization like quantization (storing weights in fewer bits) matters for shipping them.1
