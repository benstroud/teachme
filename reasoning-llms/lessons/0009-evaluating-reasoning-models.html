Evaluating Reasoning Models

Hard verifiable benchmarks, sampling settings, and test-time compute scaling.

The mental model in one sentence

To judge a reasoning model you need hard, verifiable benchmarks (AIME, MATH, GPQA, Codeforces) and you must read them at the right sampling setting — because one lucky sample and 64-sample consensus tell very different stories.1

Reading the numbers

MeasureWhat it tells you
pass@1Accuracy of a single generation — realistic for API use.1
Majority vote / consensusBest when you can sample many answers (costly).1
Reranked samplesUpper bound with a verifier/re-ranker.1

Test-time compute scaling

Research shows you can trade inference compute for accuracy: search over more candidates, or weigh steps with a verifier, instead of buying a bigger model — especially on reasoning-heavy tasks.2 Benchmark sheets now routinely report accuracy as effort or sampling rises, not as one magic number.

Key idea When a vendor quotes a score, ask: which benchmark, at what effort, and with how many samples? Comparing apples to apples is the whole game.1
