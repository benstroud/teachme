How Reasoning Is Trained

Reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards, and the train- vs test-time compute levers.

The mental model in one sentence

Reasoning is learned, not bolted on: reinforcement learning against verifiable rewards shapes a model to think long before answering, and that is what turns a fluent model into a reliable one on hard problems.1

The R1 recipe: proof that RL alone suffices

R1-Zero:  RL from cold, NO supervised fine-tuning
   base model
     + RL on verifiable rewards (math/code)
     -> long chains of thought emerge, with self-reflection + verification

DeepSeek demonstrated that pure reinforcement learning — with no human demonstration data first — could incentivize reasoning; advanced patterns like checking and re-verification emerged on their own. A later stage added a short cold-start (SFT on examples) before RL to reach useful behavior faster, then distilled the small models from the large one.1

Train-time vs test-time compute

WhereWhat it isEffect
Train-timeMore RL during trainingStronger reasoning baked in.2
Test-timeMore thinking tokens at inferenceHigher per-query accuracy.2
Key idea Both knobs raise capability, and research shows extra test-time compute can sometimes beat buying a larger model — making the thinking budget a real product decision, not a fixed cost.3
