Lesson 1 of 10Reasoning vs Non-Reasoning LLMs

The Two Kinds of Model

One engine, two behaviors: the fluent standard model and the deliberate reasoning model.

The mental model in one sentence

A standard LLM is a next-token generator: it writes the most plausible continuation quickly. A reasoning model is trained to spend extra tokens "thinking" — an internal chain of deliberation — before it commits to an answer, buying correctness at the price of latency and cost.1

Two ways to answer, one underlying engine

Standard LLMReasoning model
Before answeringEmits the continuation almost immediatelyEmits a chain of thought first
Best atFluency, style, quick recallMath, logic, planning, deep analysis
Cost profileFew tokens, low latencyMany tokens, higher latency

Why the category exists

Reasoning models emerged because evaluation-style questions (multi-step math, coding, hard logic) expose a standard model's weakness: it cannot backtrack, so one early mistake propagates. Giving the model room to deliberate — and training that deliberation with reinforcement learning — measurably lifts those hard tasks.2

Key idea Neither kind "truly understands" differently — the reasoning model spends more deliberate compute before the answer. The distinction is a training recipe plus an inference-time behavior, not a different brain.2
