ReferenceReasoning vs Non-Reasoning LLMs

Reasoning LLM Glossary

Every term in this course, in one place. Return here when a lesson uses a word you do not fully own.

The two model classes

standard LLM
A next-token predictor: generates the most plausible next token, one at a time, from the context; fluent but non-deliberate.7
reasoning model
A model trained (usually with reinforcement learning) to emit a chain of thought and "think" before its final answer.1
autoregressive generation
Producing output token by token, each conditioned on everything before it, with no searching or backtracking.7
test-time compute
Extra computation spent at inference time (more thinking tokens, search, or sampling) to improve the final answer.2

How reasoning is learned

reinforcement learning (RL)
Training that maximizes a reward signal rather than just imitating text; used to shape chain-of-thought behavior.3
verifiable reward
An objective that can be checked automatically (e.g. a correct math answer) used to score generations during RL.3
outcome reward / process reward
Reward for the final answer vs reward for each intermediate reasoning step.6
cold start
A short supervised fine-tuning stage with demonstration chains of thought before RL, to give RL a good starting point.3
distilled reasoning model
A smaller model fine-tuned on a large reasoning model's chains of thought to inherit some of its ability cheaply.3

Chain of thought

chain of thought (CoT)
Intermediate reasoning steps produced before the final answer; prompting technique later trained in as behavior.4
thinking block / reasoning content
The internal deliberation a reasoning model produces, often shown in the UI or available in the API.1
hidden CoT
A provider's choice to keep full internal reasoning private and show only a short summary (safety/competitive reasons).2

Control & behavior

reasoning effort
A parameter that sets how much thinking the model does (e.g. low/medium/high), trading latency and tokens for accuracy.1
overthinking
Spending many reasoning tokens on a trivial request where a standard model would already answer well.
routing
Choosing per-request whether to call a reasoning or a standard model, based on task difficulty, latency, and cost.1

Measuring reasoning

AIME
A hard competition-math exam used to measure reasoning; frontier scoring was ~12% (standard) rising past ~75% (reasoning).2
GPQA
A graduate-level physics/biology/chemistry benchmark; reasoning models exceed human PhD-level accuracy.2
Codeforces
Competitive programming; reasoning models reach top-percentile rankings.2
pass@1 / majority vote
Accuracy of one sample vs aggregating many samples (consensus), which improves raw pass rate.2
test-time compute scaling
The finding that spending more inference compute (or using a verifier) can improve reasoning accuracy, sometimes better than a larger model.5
