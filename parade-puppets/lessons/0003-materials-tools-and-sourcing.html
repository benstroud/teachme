Materials, Tools & Sourcing

Lesson 3 Budget-friendly focus ~15 minutes

Giant puppet building has always been a poor person's art form. Bread and Puppet Theater has made world-class puppets from river clay, recycled cardboard, and cornstarch for over 60 years. You don't need a big budget — you need to know what to look for and where to find it cheap or free.

This lesson gives you the complete shopping list for a first backpack puppet, organized by where to source each item.

The Three Categories

Every material in puppet building falls into one of three categories:

  1. Structure — the skeleton: frame, spine, crossbar, head armature
  2. Skin — the surfaces: papier-mache, foam, paint
  3. Costume — the fabric, trim, and finishing

Structure Materials

Item Purpose Where to source Approx. cost
External-frame backpack Wearable frame base Thrift store, Craigslist/FB Marketplace, garage sales $5–$20
PVC pipe, 3/4" diameter, 8 ft Spine (upright pole) Hardware store (Home Depot, Lowe's) $3–$5
PVC pipe, 1/2" diameter, 4 ft Shoulder crossbar, arm bones Hardware store $2–$3
PVC T-connectors and elbows Joining spine to crossbar Hardware store $1–$3
Wooden dowels, 3/8" diameter, 3 ft (x2) Control rods for arms Hardware store or craft store $2–$4
Hose clamps (2–3) Attaching spine to backpack frame Hardware store $3–$5
Zip ties (bag of 50+) General-purpose fastening Hardware store, dollar store $2–$4
Corrugated cardboard (large flat sheets) Head armature, hand shapes Free: Walmart, Home Depot, mattress stores, appliance stores $0
Free cardboard sources. The Cattywampus manual recommends asking at Walmart, Home Depot, Lowe's, Best Buy, and especially mattress stores for large flat pieces. Single-wall corrugated is best for sculpting; double-wall for flat surfaces. Small boxes are easy to find — the large flat sheets take a bit of hunting.

Costume Materials

Item Purpose Where to source Approx. cost
Lightweight fabric (nylon, polyester), ~12 yards Main costume body Thrift store (curtains, bedsheets), fabric store sales $5–$30
Old bedsheets (2–3) Backup fabric, under-layers Thrift store, your own linen closet $2–$6
Velcro strips (sew-on or adhesive) Closures, access panels Fabric store, dollar store $3–$5
Decorative trimmings (ribbon, felt, faux fur) Hair, details, character finishing Craft store, thrift store, fabric scraps $0–$10
Fabric is often the biggest expense. The Cattywampus manual notes that cloth is "generally the most expensive element of any giant puppet construction." Check Jo-Ann Fabrics sales, use their coupons aggressively, and always check thrift stores for large curtains or flat sheets in usable colors before buying new fabric.

Essential Tools

You likely own most of these already. The only specialized item is staple pliers.

Tool Purpose Notes
Scissors (heavy-duty) Cutting cardboard, fabric, paper A second pair of cheap scissors for glue-covered work
Staple pliers Stapling cardboard and fabric away from edges The Cattywampus manual recommends the Salco P694 Sword Point. Use 3/8" galvanized staples.
Hot glue gun + sticks Quick bonding of cardboard, foam, fabric A low-temp gun is safer for beginners
Hacksaw or PVC cutter Cutting PVC pipe to length PVC hand cutters (~$10) are cleaner and easier than a saw
Screwdriver (Phillips) Tightening hose clamps, attaching hardware
Duct tape General-purpose structural tape Not a long-term solution, but invaluable during construction
Paintbrushes (2–3 sizes) Painting, applying glue Cheap foam brushes work fine for base coats and glue
Mixing containers Papier-mache paste, paint mixing Old yogurt containers, takeout containers — anything disposable
Ruler or measuring tape Measuring pipe lengths, fabric cuts
Marker (permanent) Marking cut lines on PVC, cardboard

Budget Breakdown

Here's a realistic budget for a first 7–8 foot backpack puppet, assuming you source smart:

Category Thrift/scavenge route All-new route
Structure (frame, PVC, hardware) $15–$25 $30–$45
Skin (paper, paste, paint) $5–$15 $20–$35
Costume (fabric, trim) $10–$20 $30–$50
Tools (if buying new) $10–$20 $25–$40
Total $40–$80 $105–$170

Bread and Puppet's entire ethos is that this art form should cost almost nothing. The more you scavenge, the more authentic your puppet-building practice.

Check Your Understanding

Why does the Cattywampus manual recommend brown paper bags over newspaper for papier-mache?

Brown paper bags produce a stronger, more rigid shell than newspaper because the paper stock is thicker and more durable. This means you can use fewer layers to get the same structural rigidity — 2 to 4 layers on a cardboard armature versus more with newspaper.

What specialized tool does the Cattywampus manual specifically recommend for cardboard puppet construction?

Staple pliers (specifically the Salco P694 Sword Point) have a pointed tip that can pierce through cardboard or fabric, letting you staple anywhere — not just at the edge. This is essential for cardboard puppet construction where you need to join overlapping pieces in the middle of a surface.

Which type of corrugated cardboard is best for sculpting puppet shapes?

Single-wall corrugated cardboard is the easiest to sculpt with — it bends, curves, and folds more readily than double-wall. Double-wall is better for flat applications like signs or covering large flat surfaces. The Cattywampus manual is specific about this distinction.

Your Task

Make a sourcing run — or at least a sourcing plan:

  1. Visit a thrift store and look for external-frame backpacks. If you find one that fits, grab it.
  2. Ask a big-box store for free cardboard. Mattress stores are the best source for large flat sheets.
  3. Inventory what you already own: scissors, tape, containers, any fabric or paint.
  4. Make a shopping list for what's missing.
