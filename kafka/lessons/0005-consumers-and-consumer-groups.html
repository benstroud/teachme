Consumers & Consumer Groups

Reading partitions by offset — together with a group of peers that split the work.

The mental model in one sentence

A consumer reads partitions by tracking its offset; a consumer group is a set of consumers that each read a slice of a topic, the partitions assigned among them.12

Reading by offset

consume() -> read next at offset N -> process -> commit(N+1)

The consumer remembers its committed offset so it resumes from where it left off after a restart — or restarts from the earliest/latest if it has none.

Consumer groups

A group of consumers cooperates to read a topic: each partition is assigned to exactly one consumer in the group. So one consumer gets partition 0's events, another gets partition 1's, and together they drain the topic in parallel.1

Rebalancing

When a member joins or leaves the group, Kafka rebalances — reassigning partitions among the members. This is how scaling and failure recovery work, and why consumers should expect to occasionally lose/regain a partition.

Multiple groups read independently

Different groups each maintain their own offsets and read the full topic independently. This is the "many consumers, same events" pay-off from Lesson 1 — one topic can feed an analytics group and a notification group, each reading everything from its own position.2

The offset-commit semantics link

  • Commit after processing → if it crashes, events are reprocessed (at-least-once).
  • Commit before processing → a crash loses those events (at-most-once).

Where you commit is the lever that sets your delivery semantics (Lesson 6).

Key idea Partition = the shared unit of state (a consumer's position). Groups let many consumers scale horizontally over a topic, and where each commits its offset decides how much each is willing to replay after a crash.
