Delivery Semantics

At-most, at-least, exactly-once — and the producer + offset choices that produce each.

The mental model in one sentence

Kafka offers three delivery semantics — at-most-once, at-least-once, and exactly-once — and which one you get follows from your producer acks, your consumer offset-commits, and (for exactly-once) the idempotent producer and transactions.1

The three semantics

SemanticsMeaningHow to get it
At-most-onceRecords processed ≤ once; possible loss.Commit offset before processing; low-acks producer.
At-least-onceRecords processed ≥ once; no loss, duplicates possible.Commit after processing (the default, common choice).1
Exactly-onceRecords processed exactly once.Idempotent producer + transactions.

Why at-least-once is the default

Consumers commit their offset after processing. If the consumer crashes between processing and committing, the partition replays those events — no loss, but they can be processed again. This is why consumers are expected to handle duplicates (be idempotent).

Reaching exactly-once

Exactly-once requires two pieces: the idempotent producer (deduplicates a producer's own retries) and transactions (atomic writes to multiple partitions, and offsets committed together with the results). Together these prevent both duplicates and partial multi-partition effects.1 Exactly-once costs performance and complexity — use it when correctness genuinely demands it.

Choosing

  • At-least-once + idempotent consumers — the pragmatic standard for most pipelines.
  • Exactly-once — when a duplicate side effect is unacceptable (e.g. financial flows) and the throughput cost is acceptable.
  • At-most-once — telemetry/analytics where a missed sample is fine.
Key idea "Exactly-once" isn't free — it's built from idempotence plus transactions. For most systems, design the consumer to be idempotent and accept at-least-once; reserve exactly-once for where a duplicate truly hurts.
